Our materials

How we choose materials

Materials that last

We choose materials that can be maintained — materials whose life is not simply the life of the adhesive holding them together.

Materials with a long record

We choose building materials that have been used for generations, whose qualities are well understood, and which will go on being used for a long time yet.

Materials made from natural things

Rather than synthetic materials such as vinyl and plastic, or products bonded with synthetic adhesives, we choose building materials made from natural things that are kind to the body.

Materials that suit good furniture

Have you ever put a favourite piece of furniture into a room finished in ordinary vinyl wallcovering and plywood flooring, and felt that something did not quite sit right?
We choose materials whose texture agrees with the furniture, so that good pieces are shown at their best.

The materials we use

Lime plaster walls and ceilings

For walls and ceilings we propose shikkui lime plaster. The plaster we work with is 100% natural. Lime plaster has been used in Japan for centuries, and it is excellent for fire resistance, for regulating humidity, and for safety.
Laid by a skilled plasterer, smooth and yet full of character, a lime plaster wall does what vinyl wallcovering cannot: it takes the shadows of the lighting beautifully.

Paint

There are a fair number of places in a home that are painted — timber joinery, skirtings and so on. Paints come in many kinds, oil-based and water-based, and some cannot be used without thinning with solvent or lacquer. For paint used inside a home, we use paint free of the volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that are harmful to people.
We recommend Farrow & Ball of England, a paint with a long history. It uses natural pigments, has no smell, comes in a wide range of colours, and has been loved for generations.

Solid timber flooring

Flooring ranges from the common printed wood-grain plywood — wood waste bound with synthetic adhesive — to boards cut from a single piece of solid timber. The first has a glossy surface, and some are coated to resist scratching. They are easy to look after, but they do not last.
Solid timber flooring moves a little with the seasons, but it has depth of character and it is soft underfoot. And because the board is around 15 mm thick, a floor marked by years of use can be sanded back and brought up clean again.
We lay solid timber flooring board by board, screw-fixed, with no adhesive, and finish it in natural oil. With time the oils in the timber itself deepen the colour and the sheen.

Carpet made from natural fibres

Most carpet is synthetic — acrylic, nylon. We propose carpet in natural fibres such as wool and linen, which are warm in winter and cool in summer.
We lay it without the adhesive normally used.
