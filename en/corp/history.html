Our story

Timeless Living came out of a long search of ours for what makes a home comfortable.

How it began

What made us want to take up apartment renovation was a bitter experience of our own. We spent years at a general contractor, working on apartment construction in site management and in design, and we built more apartment buildings than we could count. We built each one as hard and as whole-heartedly as we knew how, and yet somehow there was no sense of achievement in it. If anything there was a kind of sadness.

A kind of sadness

Before handover the buyer inspects the finished flat. On one such visit the buyer's parent, who had come along, said: cheap shoji like this, and it costs tens of millions? We showed them the other rooms, and everything said about them was negative.
At another inspection, in one of the buildings where a flat costs a hundred million yen or more, the buyer arrived with a renovation contractor, tapped the walls with the flat of her hand and began discussing changes to the plan and the finishes: we can take this part out, can't we. She had bought it intending from the start to strip the interior and do it again. If that is so, we thought, we might as well have handed it over as a shell and never fitted it out at all — and what was the point of more than a year of effort alongside all those craftsmen? It took the strength out of us.
We worked as hard as we could and the customer took no pleasure in it; however hard we tried, no one was grateful. We puzzled over why that should be.

A fresh start

Looking back, the apartments we built were built to avoid complaints. Synthetic materials that do not show dirt; a plan in which the kitchen and the living room are arranged safely within a given floor area. They were, in other words, ready-made homes. We came to think that this was a long way from what people actually wanted.
What we felt most keenly then was that we wanted to see a customer look pleased. The reason the work brought no sense of achievement was that we never saw it. We decided to work in order to see that face, and set up a new organisation to do it.
And to see it, we thought, we first had to know what people wanted and then meet it. So we looked for somewhere we could hear what customers actually say — and in a new-build apartment or speculative housing company you cannot. We decided a renovation company would know best, and began working with a firm doing apartment renovations.

What satisfaction turns on

At first, vaguely, we thought it was a matter of design: if the design looked good, surely the customer would be satisfied. Then we began working with another firm and started to wonder whether that was really all there was to it.
What made us wonder was a company working in natural materials. Walk into a new apartment and there is that chemical smell particular to new building; we had always taken it for granted. In a house built of natural materials, newly finished, there is no such smell at all. Inside it we felt so well that the conversation ran on and we stayed far longer than we meant to. We had never experienced anything like it.
Asking ourselves why, we concluded it was the materials. Surrounded by natural materials, people settle, and feel at peace — oddly so. We felt in our own bodies, then, how large an effect the atmosphere a material gives off has on a person. And we saw that what the body feels with the eyes closed is something we have to convey to our clients.
There was one more thing we felt strongly. Put briefly: even inside a modern apartment with sophisticated air conditioning, it matters to work with Japan's climate.
A modern apartment, tightly sealed at the windows and finished in modern synthetic materials, is more humid in summer and drier in winter than an older house, and it puts the body under strain from volatile chemicals around the clock, without a break. A room that has forgotten the humidity, the temperature, the air, the light and the clean air Japan has always valued will not be comfortable enough, however fashionable its interior design. Real comfort in a home is being kind to the body. We did not understand that while we were designing for the eye alone.
Through a great deal of experience we arrived in the end at this: use natural materials well, and make a home of real design quality that suits Japan's climate. To design the sensations that cannot be pictured or measured. We put our effort into that, and from it made our own set of design rules — the Timeless Living concept.

The Timeless Living concept

To our pleasure, there were clients who understood it at once. They had their homes renovated on the Timeless Living concept, and several years on they are living in them very comfortably and very well. To test what the concept could do, we then deliberately went into districts full of expensive apartments, bought flats in well-known vintage blocks, stripped and renovated them, and over three years sold seven of them as renovated vintage apartments.
Being central districts of that kind, many of the people who came to view were used to expensive apartments; a great number of them thought highly of what we had done, and some asked us to renovate for them in the same way, which we have since done.
Those who bought the renovated flats did so on sight, expensive as they were, and once living in them have been very satisfied with how comfortable they are.
Timeless Living has now completed fifty homes, and the curious thing is that every client says the same: home is the most comfortable place there is, so wherever I go I want to come straight back. We feel we have made homes more comfortable than a hotel or a restaurant. In households with children, and with pets, we are told that they have become calmer and in better spirits than before — so it seems that not only people but animals feel it too.
We stay in touch with every client after the work is done, and what we look forward to above all is seeing them, ten and twenty years from now, thoroughly at ease in rooms that have become entirely their own.
