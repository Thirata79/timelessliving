The T residence　Shinagawa, Tokyo
Renovation completed 2008 (visited June 2012)

The Timeless Living home that taught us the pleasure of sleep

Mr and Mrs T run several nail salons; they dress well and have a strong sense of beauty. Mrs T has worked at the forefront of manicure for over thirty years, and her own approach to care — aimed at beautiful, healthy natural nails — and her colour technique, which draws out the quiet beauty native to Japanese hands, have won her a wide following; she is a figure of real standing in the industry. Timeless Living Shimazuyama, which they bought already renovated in April 2008, is a house with the air of a hotel: a fireplace, a bath with jets and bubbles, two basins at the washstand. Fine Scandinavian furniture, chosen by an exacting eye, sits easily in an understated and well-judged space.

— How did you come to hear of Timeless Living Shimazuyama?
Husband　There was an insert in the newspaper, drawn in a hand-made style, and it caught the eye. We looked at the details — the plan was lovely, and it came with a garden. It looks interesting, we said; shall we call in on the way to work?
Wife　We really did drop in quite casually. We had only been in our new apartment two years, and it was not as though we disliked it.
Husband　And yet within two or three days of seeing it we had decided. An older block with that interior — solid teak flooring and lime plaster walls — was very good indeed. In a new block I do not think we would have liked it the same way.
— Could you tell us in more detail how it struck you the first time?
Wife　We had been looking at nothing but new-build apartments, so it felt entirely different. For one thing, it did not feel like somewhere I had come for the first time. And I was particularly drawn to the plaster walls.
Husband　The materials are completely different. New apartments let you pick colours and patterns these days, but the materials are all the same in the end, aren't they. And it is the material itself that is wrong — one simply cannot settle.
Wife　They all seemed the same to me.
Husband　Even the show flats in expensive developments — money has been spent, perhaps, but I could not look at them and think, yes, this is good. Mostly it was: why this wallcovering? Why this floor?
Wife　Everything gleams, and one cannot settle.
Husband　Coming here taught me the merit of old things, and of traditional lime plaster and solid timber. In the old apartment, a day off spent at home made me want to go out. I could never get into the frame of mind for a long afternoon nap. Here, though, I could sleep as much as I like. At the viewing they told us one sleeps well here, and it turned out to be exactly right. I really do sleep well.
Wife　I never used to doze off either; it was only after coming to this house that I started to.
— How do you find the hotel-like fittings in use?
Husband　Going from the separate shower enclosure to the bath is a little awkward. I am rather one for the Japanese way — a quick rinse and straight into the bath. But in return, the bath itself stays clean all the time.
Wife　It does not bother me at all. I use the jet bath every single day, and it takes the tiredness out of me.
Husband　The shower floor is a stone called sabi-ishi, and it is interesting to see the colour change gradually with use. Materials that gain character over the years are apparently called growing stone, or growing wood — a pleasure we did not have in our old home.
Wife　I had always wanted a washstand with two basins, so I am delighted to have got my wish. There are only the two of us, but we tend to do things at the same time, so two is very convenient.
Husband　Neither of us gets in the other's way (laughs). Having two WCs is convenient too.
Wife　Two WCs and two basins sounds extravagant, but it makes every day comfortable and free of friction, so I do recommend it. As for the fireplace, at first I thought we would not need it; once it was actually lit, I realised how good it is simply to watch a fire.
Husband　The warmth of a flame is a different thing. Even in deep winter the underfloor heating and the fire are enough. We never use the air conditioning for heat, all year.
— How do you enjoy being at home?
Husband　I have always loved interiors, so I enjoy rearranging the rooms from time to time. In summer I set them so that the garden is easy to look at; in winter I turn the chairs towards the fire.
Wife　Living here has changed his taste in furniture.
Husband　I used to like Italian furniture. Now I prefer Scandinavian pieces, which sit better in these rooms.
Wife　The garden is mine. I started working on it properly about two years ago and it is coming along nicely. The wall of the building next door screens it just enough, so we can leave the curtains open without being overlooked, which is good.
Husband　And because the deck terrace is exactly the same as the living-room floor, inside and outside hold together, which I like.
Wife　A garden asks for a good deal of attention, but when the camellia we planted a while ago flowers I can bring some indoors, and the view will change — I am looking forward to it.
Husband　Four years on, I think the size and the plan are ideal for two people.
Wife　There is room enough to be alone when you want to be, and it is not too large. A very large place is hard work to clean.
Husband　You understand what is good about this house the moment you come in. I keep telling Timeless Living they are welcome to use ours as a show flat (laughs).
A bright, lively husband and a clever, beautiful wife. For two people whose working days are busy, what matters most in a home is being able to relax. Their house reflects that, and it is both comfortable and full of a keen sense of beauty. We expect both the interior and the garden to go on getting better.
