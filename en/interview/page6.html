The A residence　Toshima, Tokyo
Renovation completed 2012 (visited June 2015)

A dream of living in a house of lime plaster, come true

Mr A grew up in Kyoto, Mrs A in Asakusa. Both were raised among temples, shrines and houses built in lime plaster, and lime plaster was the first condition they set for their new home. With renovation already in mind they chose a seventeen-year-old apartment in the quiet residential streets of Mejiro. A full skeleton renovation by Timeless Living gave a plan that balances enclosure and openness, and with every room finished in lime plaster the result is clean and comfortable. The Scandinavian furniture and the plants they chose together suit it well.

— Could you tell us how you came to work with Timeless Living?
Wife　We were looking for a renovation company that would work in lime plaster, and the sites of the firms we found were full of projects in a sort of country-guesthouse style, which was not what we had in mind. Then an estate agent told us there was a good company, and introduced us to Timeless Living. Looking at the site, the work was so beautiful that we were almost put off approaching them — but we telephoned and went to the showroom.
Husband　We were approaching several firms at the time, and in the end narrowed it to three, Timeless Living among them, and asked each for a design. And in fact we settled on a different company first — but something about it never sat right.
So at short notice we asked Timeless Living for another meeting, and talking to them we felt again that we saw things the same way, that it fitted. We withdrew from the company we had chosen and asked Timeless Living instead. The other firm was not bad at all. They listened well — I had lived in England, and when I said I wanted the cornice moulding one often sees in English homes, they came back with the design detail in no time.
Wife　Timeless Living, on the other hand, proposed no moulding at all. This house does not need it, they said.
Husband　Even when it is what the client has asked for, if they think it is better left out they say so plainly. Only confidence allows that. In the end we dropped the moulding — and had we put it in, I think it would have been too heavy for this house and we would have regretted it.
— And how was it once the work actually started?
Husband　It felt as though we were making it together, and it was thoroughly enjoyable. They took what we had in mind and, as professionals, made it real without fuss. They put forward the best scheme for what we wanted and what we could spend, and we were grateful for that.
Wife　For instance, they recommended a less expensive solid timber for the kitchen floor than for the living room: a kitchen is bound to get dirty, and it is not a place where one can be careful. I thought they were quite right.
Husband　Even after work began they would suggest things — this door is a good one, let us repaint it and keep it — and they stayed flexible and attentive to the end. And perhaps we are simply shameless, but they have gone on advising us since the renovation finished. When we ordered a chest and told them the name of the furniture shop, they vouched for it: you will be fine there.
Wife　Perhaps our sense of what is good is close to theirs. The ceramic panels in the entrance and the bedroom we bought at an exhibition Timeless Living held at their showroom. They are by a Swedish artist; we fell for them and bought four.
— You got your original wish of lime plaster in every room. How has that been?
Wife　Timeless Living's own lime plaster exceeded what we had hoped for, and we are very glad we took it into the wardrobes and the shoe cupboard as well. It absorbs smells remarkably; even the particular smell of dry-cleaned clothes fades away if they hang in the wardrobe.
Husband　Clothes that smell of cigarettes lose it in two or three days hanging in the wardrobe, which astonished me. The shoe cupboard has no smell at all. Apparently the plaster breaks the compounds down. And because it regulates humidity, the windows do not run with condensation, which impressed me. In photographs we were shown when we bought the apartment they were streaming; now it is only a little on the aluminium frames.
Wife　The light is different, too. You see it clearly in photographs — things come out beautifully. It is apparently because the plaster scatters the light; it is enough to make you think your photography has improved.
— Apart from the plaster, what else are you pleased with?
Wife　I love the white window frames and the white wooden blinds on the balcony side. One reason we chose this home was the cherry tree right in front of the balcony, and because everything around the window is white, it shows beautifully. We eat breakfast at the dining table by the glass every morning and look at it.
Husband　Sitting in the morning sun coming through the window, gazing at the cherry and thinking of nothing, is bliss. It makes you not want to leave the house.
Wife　My husband often leaves saying, no, no, this will not do. On a free morning the two of us sit at that table for an hour or so.
Husband　You can see the cherry from the bedroom too. Leave the doors of the walk-through wardrobe between the bedroom and the living room open, and you can look at it from bed.
Wife　In full bloom it is something else.
Husband　We like the solid timber flooring too. What surprised us is that it is built so that it can be lifted and taken with us — because it is fixed not with adhesive but with screws you cannot see from above.
Wife　We are told the washstand can be taken too.
Husband　We were told of both that we could take them if we ever moved. For the moment it looks unlikely we shall make use of that. We have no intention of leaving this house at all (laughs)
Mrs A received us with an excellent home-made plum cordial; Mr A told us he enjoys watching the tits, white-eyes and starlings that come to the cherry tree. From the way they took turns to tell the story, and from the carefully chosen furniture and objects about the house, we came away with a sense of a careful, rich life, and of a couple who are close. May the home they took their time to choose and to build go on being a place of great happiness for them both.
