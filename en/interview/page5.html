The H residence　Shinagawa, Tokyo
Renovation completed 2011 (visited August 2013)

A Timeless Living renovation two years in the making

The H family are three: a couple who are both university professors, and a daughter who had just started primary school. The home, renovated over two summer holidays in 2010 and 2011, is thought through down to the kitchen cupboards, the audio system in the living and dining room, and the height of every door handle. Design and function meet exactly.

— What led you to ask Timeless Living to renovate?
Wife　We were actually looking for somewhere to move to. We came to this apartment twelve years ago, when it was new, but we were unhappy with the floor and wall finishes, and my husband wanted a large living room, so from time to time we went to look at places that seemed promising.
Husband　One day a leaflet for a renovated apartment came through the door, and the agent handling it turned out to be Mr K, whom we had known for some time. It was nearby, so we thought we would go and look — and it was an apartment Timeless Living had done. The interior astonished us; it was beautiful.
Wife　Lime plaster ceilings and walls, timber-framed windows — I had never seen such things in an apartment. The house I grew up in was built in lime plaster and solid timber, so I had long wanted an interior like that, but I had assumed it was impossible in an apartment. I had also been convinced that solid timber could not be used over underfloor heating, so learning that it could was a happy surprise.
Husband　I was moved that a renovation could go that far. But nothing came of it at the time; we went on looking at other places and never found one that felt right.
Wife　When we had rather run out of ideas, it struck me, and I said to my husband: we need not move at all — why not just take out the wall between the kitchen and the living and dining room? He was all for it, so we got in touch with Mr K straight away and arranged to meet Timeless Living. That was May 2011, and work began two months later, in July.
Husband　In New York, where we lived for about five years, pre-war buildings are worth more, and renovating when you move in is taken for granted. So the idea of spending money renovating an older apartment held no difficulty for us. Wanting a large living room came from the apartment we had in New York, too.
— You had the work done in two phases, I believe?
Wife　We did not want to move out while the work was done, and our work gives us long summer holidays, so we asked whether it could be done then. They proposed doing it in two phases. Hearing that, I thought: here is a company one can trust. People usually worry about aftercare with a renovation, and splitting the work in two means that if anything goes wrong in the first phase it will be put right — and that they said it at all suggested they were confident nothing would go wrong. And I was not mistaken.
Husband　Three summers ago, the kitchen and washroom; two summers ago, the living room, entrance and hallway. About five weeks each time, and we went up to the mountains as a family while the work was on.
— What were the key points of the renovation?
Wife　The main aims were lime plaster walls and solid teak flooring, and taking out walls and the storeroom to make an open kitchen and a large living room. Happily we liked the kitchen from the maker Timeless Living recommended and settled it easily; but we went back and forth a great deal over storage for the china, and for the cooking equipment, vacuum cleaner and household things that had been in the storeroom. How much capacity? What layout? Less storage means more room to relax in, so we worked out the widths and depths carefully, thinking about how we move around. In the end we put full-height storage along the wall behind the kitchen, with flat teak doors.
Husband　We were advised that thin teak doors would be overwhelmed by the weight of the stainless-steel kitchen, and that thicker ones would be better. It cost more, but once it was built I could see they were right.
Wife　Putting a Linn network audio system into the living and dining room was a big thing too. My husband is quite taken with it.
Husband　I often work at the dining table with music on, so I had wanted a good system; I heard Linn at Timeless Living's open house, liked it very much, and we put one in. Network audio is a new, digital kind of system, and the point of it is that you can listen at studio-master resolution. It is nothing like a CD — it comes to the ear clearly. The ceiling speakers are not high-performance ones, but for listening while working they are just right.
Wife　The entrance was another key point. We had liked the open house design there too, so we asked for the same: a limestone wall and a display shelf facing the door. For the shelf, which has a drawer, full-size mock-ups of different lengths and heights were fixed to the actual wall, and we went through them one by one with Timeless Living and decided together.
Husband　We wanted marble on the entrance floor, and the proposal for how to lay it surprised us. The slabs would be cut into 100 mm squares, and set with a slight step between neighbouring pieces, so that light from above would throw shadows across the stone. The reason was that in a space with so many strong things in it — the limestone wall, the solid teak floor, the large glass door, the antique carpets we collect — a flat, uniform marble floor would be overwhelmed.
Wife　To decide the steps we shone a light on a mock-up together and studied which pieces to raise. When it was finished and we saw the stones floating in the teak floor, we felt it had been worth it. For the glass door in the hallway they proposed a low handle position on design grounds, and seeing it simulated in place we agreed — it does look better. Having things explained with full-size mock-ups, again and again, was genuinely valuable. It brought home how much it matters to experience the parts a drawing cannot convey. And in small ways too: the washstand has a magnifying mirror and a drawer specifically for pegs and hangers — it reaches exactly the places one wants reached. We are grateful to Timeless Living for staying with us right down to the smallest detail.
— Two years on from completion, how is it to live in?
Husband　The floor has taken on a sheen and is coming along nicely. A cordless vacuum is enough for the cleaning, which is easier than it was with carpet.
Wife　The open kitchen joined to the living and dining room is very good — I feel connected to my husband and my daughter. Though I am told that while the television is on, the dishwasher and the extractor can be a little intrusive.
Husband　That much is unavoidable, I suppose. I had worried about the ventilation itself, but that seems to be fine. I used to work in my own room sometimes; now I work nowhere but the enlarged living and dining room. I sit beside my daughter while she does her homework, working with a symphony or some jazz on.
A husband who says he is a fearsome teacher at work but is plainly very gentle with his daughter, and a wife who smiles easily and loves interiors. Their renovation drew beautifully on the house she grew up in and on their years in New York. No doubt they will go on enjoying changing the home as their daughter grows.
