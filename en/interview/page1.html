The A residence　Shibuya, Tokyo
Renovation completed 2008 (visited November 2009)

Meeting Timeless Living changed the way we live

Mr and Mrs A are a close couple who work in Tokyo and keep busy days. They were living in a newly built apartment they had only just bought when they happened to see Timeless Living Hiroo, already renovated, and fell for it on the spot. They bought it in 2008. In the living room, where the woodland of Hiroo opens up beyond the window, they have put a timber dining table by the glass, and there they enjoy an easy daily life — a glass of the wine they love, a sense of being at one with what is outside, the shadows the lighting casts across the plaster.

— Could you start by telling us how you first came across Timeless Living?
Yes. My elder sister moved to Hiroo, and I wondered what sort of place Hiroo was, so I searched online — and happened on the website. That was the first time I came across Timeless Living.
“Is there really an apartment like this in Japan?” I still remember clearly how the photographs on the site struck me. I called my wife over at once and the two of us sat there staring at them, and we simply had to see it for ourselves — I telephoned the next day and arranged a viewing.
— Were you planning to move?
No. We had bought a new apartment and lived in it about three years, so moving on was nowhere in our minds.
But my wife is very interested in interiors, and deciding what to do with them was a pleasure we shared — and in that new apartment we somehow never quite managed to say what we meant. We would buy a lovely antique piece, or a clock, and once it was in the room something was not right. We had almost given up on it, thinking that lovely old furniture and objects with years behind them simply do not suit a new Japanese apartment.
— What did you think when you saw Timeless Living Hiroo?
The moment I stepped into the entrance, wrapped in solid timber flooring and lime plaster walls, I genuinely caught my breath. And going on into the rooms I was filled with the happiness of having finally found the home we had been looking for all that time. Soft sunlight came in, the air was clear — how to put it — as though time ran slowly in that one place. After that I was so absorbed in looking round that I forgot even to speak to my wife, but I could tell perfectly well that she had fallen for Timeless Living Hiroo too (laughs).
During the viewing the Timeless Living planner explained each material and each design decision, and I could feel the reasons for the comfort of that room settling into place one by one. Put simply, what makes a space like that is natural materials chosen down to the last detail, and the care the designers and craftspeople take in setting them out. I thought: real luxury is here.
— Did you decide to buy straight away?
It is a large purchase, so I tried to tell myself to think it over carefully — but I was completely taken over by the feeling that everything I value, everything I respond to, was concentrated in that one apartment. There is only one of it; nothing else would do. If we let this go we would never get it again. I simply wanted to live there. For a while my wife and I talked about nothing but Timeless Living, every day.
Once we had decided to buy, the Timeless Living planner talked everything through with us. Looking back, it was a thoroughly enjoyable time. We came to admire everyone's eye there, and by now we are great fans of them all (laughs).
The way we live changed
— What changed once you were actually living there?
We had a study in the old apartment too, but it was tacked on to a corner of a room that got no sun. Because of that there was always a struggle before I went in — I had to work myself up to it, right, time to get on with it — and although it was the same home it felt somehow cut off. At Timeless Living Hiroo the study, not just the living room, is designed as a principal room, and the two are beautifully in accord. Now I look forward to going into my study. I think a great many men would like a study like it. These days I spend half an hour in there after I get up, before I leave. I used to sleep until the last possible minute and rush out to work. The way I use my time has changed entirely.
— In what way has the use of your time changed?
Rather than being driven by time as I used to be, I have come to hold the reins — to decide deliberately how my time is spent.
I spend more time at home doing nothing in particular, and I think I have learned to set up the moments that make me feel well. In the daytime, the sense of being at one with what is outside the window; in the evening, the contrast between the light and the shadows it throws across the plaster, or an hour of bliss with the wine I like, some olives and bread. Come to think of it, it was only after moving here that I learned the names of trees and birds.
— It sounds rather like living at a resort.
I have never been to Karuizawa, but perhaps it is something like this. People go out to restaurants, or travel, looking for unhurried time — here that comfort is simply part of the ordinary day.
— Has anything else changed?
My wife has become kinder, perhaps? We have stopped arguing, too (laughs).
