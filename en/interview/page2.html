The F residence　Shinjuku, Tokyo
Renovation completed 2005 (visited March 2012)

The first renovation built on the Timeless Living idea

Mr and Mrs F are a very close couple who make jam and do voluntary work together. In 2005 they had Timeless Living renovate their apartment, then nineteen years old. The living room where we spoke is a modern one: white lime plaster on walls and ceiling, flooring in a dark brown close to black, a glass table and black chairs. The engravings Mr F fell for and bought while posted to England, and the Lalique pieces Mrs F is fond of, are set about the room — a fine space in which you feel the taste and the eye of them both.

— What made you decide to renovate?
Husband　We moved in when the building was new, and nineteen years on the whole place had aged.
Wife　It was carpeted, and I had begun to worry about dust mites.
Husband　The wallcovering had got old and was lifting a little, and we thought it was about time we renovated.
Wife　Though people told us it still looked perfectly good and it would be a waste.
Husband　At first we only thought of changing the floor to timber and having the walls made good. But as we talked with Timeless Living we realised there were several things we really did want to put right — opening the kitchen out to face the room, replacing a cupboard that was awkward to use. Around then our daughter married and a room came free, so we also wanted to change the plan and give each of us a room of our own. And so it came to this: if we were going to do all of it, then a full skeleton renovation.
Wife　When we started going through stacks of brochures and getting specific, they proposed a renovation built in lime plaster and solid timber, with no additives at all — no synthetic adhesive. Chemicals had been on my mind for a long time, so I was delighted: is there really something as good as that? We try to keep additives out of how we live, so if the renovation could be free of them too, nothing could be better.
Husband　The cost was not much different either, so we thought, well, why not — and that is how it went ahead.
— Now that you have lived with them, how are the additive-free floors and walls?
Wife　I had Catherine Memmi, the Paris interiors house, in mind, and I wanted a dark floor. They proposed solid timber brought to a dark brown with persimmon tannin and red ochre, and I liked it very much. Seven years on it has come to a good colour. It has worn paler in places, but I like that. A floor that is not pristine feels as though people live on it, does it not? Dust shows more than it did with carpet, but then I clean more often, and I suspect that is better for one's health.
Husband　The plaster walls are warm — partly, I think, because they put proper insulation in.
Wife　We use the heating and cooling far less than we used to. You do not need to leave it running. And the walls show the dirt surprisingly little — apparently because they carry no static, so nothing is drawn to them. Vinyl wallcovering yellows and gradually looks old; it is a pleasure that plaster does not do that. And a room in lime plaster is a very restful place to be.
— What were the key points in the kitchen, and how do you find it now?
Husband　We turned it to face the room, changed from gas to induction, and had a large dresser built in. Taking out the wall between it and the living room made the space larger, and I like how the whole thing balances.
Wife　When the wall was there, an unexpected visitor could be dealt with by pulling one curtain across, which was convenient (laughs).
Husband　But then one lets it get untidy, doesn't one.
Wife　I never liked the smell of gas, so we took the chance to move to induction. If you leave it on by mistake it switches itself off after an hour, which is reassuring. And it will go down to the very lowest heat, which suits anything that has to be reduced. Only yesterday my husband and I spent four hours making kumquat jam and kumquats in syrup.
Husband　Gas in this house is down to the bath now.
Wife　We asked for the dresser to be built in because my ideal is an uncluttered room with nothing scattered about — if I could, I would live with no furniture at all. We do have a little, but my husband says that if it looked like a show flat there would be no warmth in it, so I think this is about right.
Husband　The shelves did not budge, even in the earthquake.
— What became of the cupboard that was awkward to use?
Husband　They turned it into a walk-in wardrobe, and it is far easier to use than it was.
Wife　Though reaching the things up high is a bit of a business.
Husband　To fit more in we had two hanging rails put in, and because the space is narrow the upper one ended up rather high. So I stand on a step to reach it. But we swap things round by season, and what we are wearing now is on the lower rail, so day to day it hardly matters.
— And how has it been, each of you having a room of your own?
Husband　I think it is the greatest benefit of the renovation. We used to share a bedroom, but my wife is rather a night owl and I am an early riser. Even the television in the bedroom — we wanted different programmes, or one of us would be asleep when the other wanted to watch. All of that was solved at a stroke. We used to share one computer, which lived in the living room and made the space the family uses smaller; now we each have one in our own room. The cables that used to snake across the carpet were taken under the floor, which tidied everything up.
Wife　It really is good. I can enjoy an online auction whenever I please (laughs). And we were glad to be shown several plans for where the rooms would go, so that we could choose one we were happy with.
Husband　I think of this house as finished now. I cannot find anything I really want to change.
A gentle, thoughtful husband and a bright, sociable wife. Our impression was that the renovation had made life more comfortable and richer for them both. We hope they go on enjoying full and happy days, valuing both the hours they spend alone and the hours they spend together.
