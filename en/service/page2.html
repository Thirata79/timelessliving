Q. Do you not use diatomaceous earth plaster?
Diatomaceous earth and lime plaster are often confused. Diatomaceous earth cannot set by itself and has no binding power of its own, so it is mixed with a synthetic adhesive. Lime plaster, by contrast, sets on its own when water is added, as cement does, and goes on hardening as the years pass. A binder is used to stop it cracking as it does so — but the Mutenka Jutaku lime plaster we use contains, as its name says, no additives at all: no synthetic adhesive, only natural binders such as seaweed. For design that the body registers as comfortable, we recommend lime plaster with no synthetic adhesive on the large surfaces, the walls and ceilings.

Q. Do you not use vinyl wallcovering?
Vinyl wallcovering builds up static, which draws dirt to it, so it grows dirty over time. And if it burns, in a fire, it gives off dioxins and other harmful substances. We recommend materials that carry little static and release nothing harmful — lime plaster, and paper wallcoverings.
Back to top
Q. Do you not lay stone in living rooms and hallways?
Stone conducts heat straight out of the body. In a Japanese household, where shoes come off at the door, we believe that tells on the body over the years, and so we do not recommend it.
Back to top
Q. Is there aftercare?
We issue a warranty certificate on completion and provide a one-year aftercare period. If something goes wrong after that period we deal with it all the same. With the clients we have worked for, the relationship has continued long after they moved in.
